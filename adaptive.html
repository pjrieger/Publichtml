Slide 2: Physical Restrictions & Rider Profiles

  • Spinal Cord Injuries (SCI)
    • Paraplegia or quadriplegia resulting in full lower-body paralysis.
    • Zero trunk control requiring seat harnesses and lateral supports.
  • Amputations & Neurological Conditions
    • Cerebral palsy, stroke recovery, or missing limbs affecting balance.
    • Spasticity or limited reach requiring single-sided handlebar controls.
  • Sensory & Cognitive Impairments
    • Visual impairment requiring tandem pilot guidance.
    • Mild cognitive limitations requiring clear, direct verbal instructions.

Slide 3: Common Parkway Equipment

  • Arm-Powered Drivetrains
    • Handcycles (recumbent or knee-seated) driven by hand cranks.
  • 3-Wheeled Recumbents
    • Tadpole (2 wheels front) and Delta (2 wheels rear) trikes.
    • High stability with extremely low ground clearance.
  • Tandems & Power Assists
    • Adaptive tandems with sighted pilots upfront and stokers behind.
    • E-assist mid-drive motors helping riders negotiate levee grades.

Slide 4: Solo Adaptive Cyclists

  • Trail Presence Reality
    • Disabled riders frequently cycle solo without support vehicles.
    • High level of athletic independence on paved Parkway trails.
  • Trailside Vulnerability
    • Incapable of self-extrication if tipped over or mechanically broken down.
    • Incapable of walking to a trailhead if stranded.
  • Patrol Action Protocol
    • Always perform a verbal pass-by check: "Patrol check—all good or need help?"

Slide 5: Medical Precautions vs. Able-Bodied

  • Altered Pain Feedback
    • SCI riders feel zero pain below their level of spinal injury.
    • Must perform thorough visual inspections for bleeding, road rash, or fractures.
  • Equipment & Bracing Protection
    • Custom orthoses, braces, and prosthetics are molded medical devices.
    • Do not remove prosthetics or braces unless life safety demands it.
  • Baseline Mental Status
    • Check with on-scene caregivers to establish baseline mental response.

Slide 6: Critical Alert: Autonomic Dysreflexia (AD)

  • High-Risk Population
    • Occurs in cyclists with spinal cord injuries at or above the T-6 level.
  • Triggers & Physiological Root
    • Caused by unseen painful stimuli below injury line (pinched skin, full bladder, tight harness).
    • Triggers uncontrolled sympathetic reflex causing dangerous blood pressure spikes.
  • Symptoms & Patrol Response
    • Symptoms: Severe headache, facial flushing, chest sweating, goosebumps.
    • Action: Treat as Hurry Case -> Sit rider upright -> Remove tight straps/pinches -> Call 911.

Slide 7: Common Mechanical Breakdowns

  • Low Drivetrain Clearance
    • Recumbent rear derailleurs sit inches off asphalt, striking debris and gravel.
  • Extended Chain Systems
    • Double or triple-length chains drop, twist, or break under heavy hand-crank load.
  • Complex Steering Assemblies
    • Combined steering, shifting, and braking on moving handlebar clusters lose tension.

Slide 8: Trailside Seated Repairs

  • In-Chassis Repair Workflow
    • Perform tube changes, chain fixes, and adjustments while rider remains seated.
    • Avoid transferring rider unless chassis frame is completely destroyed.
  • Vehicle Stabilization
    • Lock parking brakes or block wheels before touching drivetrain.
    • Never tip or tilt a trike while rider is strapped inside.
  • Verbal Clearance
    • Explicitly ask: "May I hold your frame or work on your drive wheel?"

Slide 9: Patrol Etiquette & Care Refusal

  • Communication Etiquette
    • Speak directly to the cyclist rather than addressing only their caregiver.
    • Respect athlete's knowledge of their own custom machinery.
  • Refusal of Assistance (PBJ)
    • Apply Patroller’s Best Judgment if an injured rider refuses medical care.
    • Step back, monitor safely from a distance, and secure witness signatures on refusal cards.

Slide 10: Summary & Checklist

  • 4-Point Operational Summary
    • Expect solo riders: Perform regular verbal check-ins on trail.
    • Inspect visually: Do not rely on pain feedback for SCI riders.
    • Watch for AD: Sit upright and remove tight straps if T-6 rider shows flushing/headache.
    • Fix in place: Work on mechanicals with rider strapped in chassis.
